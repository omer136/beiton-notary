# Notarial Power of Attorney in Israel — Types, Requirements & Fees (2026)

A notarial power of attorney (POA) is a POA that a notary prepared, or on which a notary authenticated the signatures. In it, one person (the "principal") authorizes another (the "agent") to act on their behalf before banks, government agencies, the Land Registry (Tabu), and other bodies. Under Section 19 of the Notaries Law, 1976 (חוק הנוטריונים, תשל"ו-1976), a notary's certificate is **sufficient evidence** in legal proceedings, without the need for additional proof, of the notary's statements and acts and of the statements and acts of others made or done before the notary, all as stated in the certificate.

This guide explains the main types of notarial POA, when the law requires a notary, the fees set by the regulations, and what to bring to the signing.

## Types of Notarial Power of Attorney

### General Power of Attorney

Authorizes the agent to perform a wide range of actions on behalf of the principal — managing bank accounts, handling tax matters, signing documents, and more. Ideal for individuals living abroad who need someone to manage their affairs in Israel.

### Real Estate Power of Attorney (Tabu)

A specific POA for real estate transactions, such as buying, selling, or registering rights. Under Section 20(a) of the Notaries Law, a POA for real estate transactions that require registration in the Land Registry is valid only if a notary prepared it or authenticated the signatures on it; this does not derogate from Section 91 of the Israel Bar Association Law, 1961 (חוק לשכת עורכי הדין, תשכ"א-1961). A POA given to a bank for a mortgage transaction is subject to a different rule, set out below.

### Irrevocable Power of Attorney

A POA that cannot be revoked without the consent of the agent. Common in real estate transactions — for example, a seller signs an irrevocable POA in favor of the buyer to ensure the transaction is registered even if the seller changes their mind.

### Power of Attorney for Banking & Mortgages

Under Section 20(a1) of the Notaries Law, added by Amendment 12 (2026), a POA given to a banking corporation for a mortgage transaction, including its cancellation, transfer, a change in its terms, or any other action involved in registering the transaction, is valid even if a notary did not prepare it or authenticate the signatures on it. The law no longer requires a notary for such a POA, although a bank or another body may still ask for notarial authentication in practice. For other banking actions, such as adding or removing authorized signatories or managing an account for someone who cannot visit the branch, the bank may ask for a notarial POA, often in wording it sets in advance.

### Power of Attorney for Use Abroad

A POA intended for presentation to authorities abroad. Whether an [apostille](/en/blog/apostille-israel) is also needed is decided by the receiving authority or country. Under Section 45(a) of the Notaries Law, a notary's signature on a document intended for use outside Israel is authenticated, at the request of an interested person, by a magistrates' court registrar or a state employee appointed for that purpose by the Minister of Justice.

## When Is a Notarial Power of Attorney Required?

Not every POA needs to be notarized. In some cases the law requires it; in others the requirement comes from the receiving body:

- **General POA and POA for real estate transactions requiring registration**: under Section 20(a), valid only if a notary prepared them or authenticated the signatures on them (without derogating from Section 91 of the Israel Bar Association Law)
- **POA to a bank for a mortgage transaction**: since Amendment 12 (2026) the law does not require a notary (Section 20(a1)), although the bank may ask for one in practice
- **Documents for use abroad** (embassies, foreign authorities, courts abroad): under Section 8, signature authentication on a document needed for use abroad, including before a foreign state's representation in Israel, may be done only by a notary
- **Irrevocable POA** — notarization is required by law
- **When the receiving body demands it**: banks, insurance companies, pension funds, etc.

## Notarial Power of Attorney Fees in Israel (2026)

Fees are set by the Notaries Regulations (Service Fees), 1978 (תקנות הנוטריונים (שכר שירותים), תשל"ט-1978) and are uniform nationwide.

| Service | Fee |
|---------|-----|
| First signatory on the POA | 197 ₪ |
| Each additional signatory | 77 ₪ |
| Additional certified copy | 77 ₪ |
| Revocation of POA: notice and registration of a note (item 7A(a)) | 209 ₪ |
| Certified copy with revocation note (item 7A(b)) | 74 ₪ |
| Notarial certificate in a foreign language other than Arabic or English (item 12) | 104 ₪ |

All prices are **before VAT (18%)**. Fees are uniform as regulated by the Ministry of Justice.

**Example:** POA with two signatories + additional copy = 197 + 77 + 77 = **351 ₪ + VAT = 414.18 ₪**

## Required Documents

To obtain a notarial power of attorney, bring the following:

1. **Valid ID** of the principal (the person granting the POA) — Israeli ID card with supplement, or passport
2. **Agent's details** — full name and ID number
3. **POA wording** — if the receiving body requires a specific format (e.g., a bank or the Tabu)
4. **Land Registry extract** — if the POA relates to real estate
5. **Passport** — if the POA is intended for use abroad

The agent does not need to be present at the signing.

## Signing Before the Notary

Under Section 11 of the Notaries Law, a notary may not authenticate a person's signature on a document unless the signer stood before the notary, was identified, and signed the document before the notary. Under Regulation 19A(1) of the Notaries Regulations, 1977, a person required to sign before the notary signs by hand or by electronic signature. An electronic notarial certificate does not remove the duty to stand before the notary.

## Notarial Power of Attorney for Use Abroad: Apostille

After the notarial certificate, a notary's signature on a document intended for use outside Israel is authenticated, at the request of an interested person, by a magistrates' court registrar or a state employee appointed for that purpose by the Minister of Justice (Section 45(a) of the Notaries Law). This is the route to an [apostille](/en/blog/apostille-israel) on a notarial document.

Whether an apostille is needed is decided by the authority or country where the POA will be presented, so check with the receiving body abroad in advance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Question: How much does a notarial power of attorney cost in Israel?**
Answer: 197 ₪ before VAT for the first signatory, 77 ₪ for each additional signatory. The fee is uniform nationwide and set by the Notaries Regulations (Service Fees), 1978 (תקנות הנוטריונים).

**Question: Does the agent need to be present at the notary's office?**
Answer: No. Only the principal (the person granting the POA) must appear before the notary and sign. The agent does not need to be present.

**Question: What is the difference between a regular and a notarial power of attorney?**
Answer: A notarial POA is one that a notary prepared, or on which a notary authenticated the signatures. Under Section 20(a) of the Notaries Law, a general POA and a POA for real estate transactions requiring registration are not valid without this (without derogating from Section 91 of the Israel Bar Association Law). Under Section 19, the notary's certificate is sufficient evidence in legal proceedings of the notary's statements and acts and of the statements and acts of others made or done before the notary, all as stated in the certificate. Banks and bodies abroad may set their own requirements.

**Question: How long is a notarial power of attorney valid?**
Answer: A notarial POA remains valid as long as it has not been revoked, unless an expiration date is specified in the document. An irrevocable POA cannot be canceled without the agent's consent.

**Question: Can a notarial power of attorney be revoked?**
Answer: Yes, except for an irrevocable POA. Revocation is done by notifying the notary (209 ₪, item 7A(a)) and it is advisable to also notify the agent and relevant parties directly.

**Question: Do I need an apostille on a POA for use abroad?**
Answer: That is decided by the authority or country where the POA will be presented. If it is needed, the notary's signature is authenticated, on request, by a magistrates' court registrar or a state employee appointed for that purpose by the Minister of Justice (Section 45(a) of the Notaries Law).
