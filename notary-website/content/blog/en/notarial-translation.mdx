# Notarial Translation in Israel — What You Need to Know Before You Translate

A notarial translation is a translation of a document whose correctness a notary has certified. Authorities, banks, and bodies abroad sometimes require a notarial translation before they accept a document in another language, for example an Israeli document in a foreign language or a foreign document in Hebrew.

This guide explains when a notary may certify a translation, the fee according to the [2026 notary price list](/en/blog/notary-pricing-2026), and which common documents are translated.

## What Is a Notarial Translation?

A notarial translation is a translation accompanied by a notarial certification — an official document in which the notary declares that the translation is faithful and accurate to the original. The notarial certification constitutes **sufficient evidence** under Section 19 of the Notaries Law, 1976 (חוק הנוטריונים, תשל"ו-1976).

Under Section 15 of the Notaries Law, a notary may not certify the correctness of a translation unless the notary masters both the language of the original and the language into which it was translated, and the notary personally prepared the translation or checked its correctness.

In practice, there are two paths to a translation certificate, and in both the notary must master both languages:

### Path 1: A Translator Translates, and the Notary Checks and Certifies

A translator translates the document, and then the notary checks the correctness of the translation and certifies it. On this path too, the notary may not certify a translation in a language the notary does not master.

- **Fee:** under item 3(a) of the Notaries Regulations (Service Fees), 1978 (תקנות הנוטריונים (שכר שירותים), תשל"ט-1978), by word count

### Path 2: The Notary Translates the Document Personally

The notary prepares the translation and also certifies it.

- **Fee:** the same item 3(a). Item 3 has no addition for the notary translating personally

### When the Notary Does Not Master One of the Languages

In that case the notary may not certify the correctness of the translation. The usual route is a [translator's declaration](/en/services/translator-declaration) before a notary: the translator declares that the translation is correct, and the notary receives and certifies the declaration (Section 7(5) of the Notaries Law).

## Notarial Translation Fees in Israel (2026)

Fees are set by the Notaries Regulations (Service Fees), 1978 (תקנות הנוטריונים (שכר שירותים), תשל"ט-1978) and are uniform nationwide.

### Translation Certification Fee (Item 3)

| Service | Fee |
|---------|-----|
| First 100 words | 251 ₪ |
| Each additional 100 words (up to 1,000 words) | 197 ₪ |
| Each additional 100 words (over 1,000 words) | 99 ₪ |
| Additional certified copy | 77 ₪ |

### Surcharges

| Surcharge | Fee |
|-----------|-----|
| Notarial certificate in a foreign language other than Arabic or English (item 12) | +104 ₪ |

The item 12 addition depends on the language in which the notarial certificate is drawn up, not on the language of the document.

Other services carry an addition for translation by the notary: where signature authentication (item 1(e)), a will (item 4(d)), an affidavit (item 6(d)), or authentication of a prenuptial agreement (item 11(c)) involves translation of the document by the notary, half of the item 3(a) fee is added, according to the number of words in the document. A translation certificate under item 3 itself has no such addition.

All prices are **before VAT (18%)**. Fees are uniform as regulated by the Ministry of Justice.

## Common Documents That Require Notarial Translation

### Civil Status & Registration Documents

- **Birth certificate** — for immigration, registration abroad, citizenship applications
- **Marriage / divorce certificate** — for registration in another country
- **Death certificate** — for inheritance proceedings abroad

### Academic Documents

- **Matriculation certificate** — for studying abroad
- **University degree** — for degree recognition, employment abroad
- **Transcript** — for university admissions

### Legal Documents

- **Power of attorney**: for use abroad (an [apostille](/en/blog/apostille-israel) is sometimes also required)
- **Court judgments** — for enforcement in another country
- **Agreements** — prenuptial agreements, partnership agreements

### Business Documents

- **Certificate of incorporation** — for opening a bank account abroad
- **Business license** — for business activity in another country
- **Financial statements** — for foreign tax authorities

## Available Languages

Under Section 15, a notary may certify a translation only between languages the notary masters: the language of the original and the language into which the document was translated, and only if the notary prepared the translation or checked its correctness. Where a translation is needed in other languages, the usual route is a [translator's declaration](/en/services/translator-declaration) before a notary.

## Notarial Translation and Apostille

If the translated document is intended for use outside Israel, the receiving authority or country may also require an [apostille](/en/blog/apostille-israel). The order is:

1. **Translation certificate:** the notary certifies the correctness of the translation
2. **Authentication of the notary's signature:** on request, by a magistrates' court registrar or a state employee appointed for that purpose by the Minister of Justice (Section 45(a) of the Notaries Law)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Question: How much does a notarial translation cost in Israel?**
Answer: The price depends on the document length. Under item 3(a): up to 100 words 251 ₪, each additional 100 words up to 1,000 words 197 ₪, and each 100 words above 1,000 words 99 ₪. There is no addition for the notary translating personally. All prices before VAT (18%). To calculate: [the calculator on the home page](/en).

**Question: What is the difference between a notarial translation and a regular translation?**
Answer: A regular translation is not accompanied by a notarial certificate. In a notarial translation, the notary certifies the correctness of the translation, and under Section 19 of the Notaries Law the certificate is sufficient evidence in legal proceedings of the notary's statements and acts, all as stated in the certificate. Whether a regular translation is enough or a notarial one is needed is decided by the receiving body.

**Question: How long does a notarial translation take?**
Answer: It depends on the length of the document, the languages, and whether the notary translates personally or checks a translator's work.

**Question: Do I need to bring the original document?**
Answer: Under Section 15 of the Notaries Law, a notary certifies a translation only if the notary prepared it or checked its correctness. If a certificate that a copy is a true copy of the original is also needed, under Section 12 the original must be presented to the notary, who compares the two. What is needed in each case, original or copy, also depends on the requirements of the receiving body.

**Question: Does a notarial translation also need an apostille?**
Answer: Not always. That is decided by the authority or country where the document will be presented. If it is needed, the notary's signature on the certificate is authenticated, on request, by a magistrates' court registrar or a state employee appointed for that purpose by the Minister of Justice (Section 45(a)). Read more in the [apostille guide](/en/blog/apostille-israel).

**Question: Can any notary translate from any language?**
Answer: No. Under Section 15 of the Notaries Law, a notary certifies the correctness of a translation only if the notary masters both the source and the target language, and only if the notary personally prepared the translation or checked its correctness. This applies also when a translator did the translation. For other languages, the usual route is a translator's declaration before a notary. The item 3(a) fee is the same on both paths, with no addition.
